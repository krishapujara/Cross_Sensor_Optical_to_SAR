# Scientific Limitations & Honest Boundaries
**Project:** Cross-Sensor Image Translation (Optical-to-SAR)  
**Target:** ISRO / URSC Research Demonstration

## 1. Physical vs. Statistical Radar Sensing

> **SCIENTIFIC DISTINCTION:**  
> A synthetic SAR image generated by a deep neural network is a **statistical visual approximation**. It is **NOT** a physical radar acquisition.

Real SAR backscatter ($\sigma^0$) is governed by:
1. **Radar Wavelength:** C-band ($5.6\,\text{cm}$) or L-band ($24\,\text{cm}$).
2. **Dielectric Permittivity ($\epsilon$):** Liquid moisture content dramatically alters backscatter.
3. **Surface Roughness:** Roughness at centimeter scales controls diffuse vs. specular reflection.
4. **Acquisition Geometry:** Side-looking radar geometry ($20^\circ - 45^\circ$) produces layover, foreshortening, and shadow.

A deep learning model translating an optical image has access only to visible surface reflectance from a near-nadir satellite perspective. It cannot measure soil moisture beneath a dry crust, nor can it physically penetrate dense forest canopies.

## 3. Hardware Compute Constraints

* Deep generative image-to-image translation models (U-Net with 54 million parameters) require significant computation for large-batch, high-epoch training.
* On CPU hardware, training must be restricted to small batches ($B=2$) and small representative subsets (5–20 pairs) for responsive demonstration. Full-scale training should leverage NVIDIA CUDA GPUs.
